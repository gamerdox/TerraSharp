# SH-304 Interactive Demo & Walkthrough Guide

The **SH-304 System** features a 100% deterministic, offline **DEMO MODE** that runs seamlessly without external API credentials or internet access. This guide provides a step-by-step tour of the operational prototype.

---

## Scenario 1: Wayanad District, Kerala (Western Ghats Monsoon Deluge)

### Context
In late July 2024, extreme orographic monsoon downpours struck the steep western escarpment of Wayanad, triggering catastrophic debris flows affecting Mundakkai, Chooralmala, and Meppadi.

### Walkthrough Steps:
1. **Launch Dashboard:** Open `http://localhost:5173`. Ensure the AOI dropdown shows **Wayanad District, Kerala**.
2. **Observe Hazard Layers:**
   - Click **Landslide Risk**: Notice the bright red/orange critical hazard corridor concentrated along the steep western escarpment ridge (Mundakkai, Chooralmala, Meppadi).
   - Click **Flash-Flood Risk**: Notice how the hazard shifts to the valley floors and drainage channels where upstream contributing runoff pools.
   - Click **Slope (°)**: Observe the 30m DEM-derived terrain gradient reaching $35^\circ$–$48^\circ$ on the upper scarps.
   - Click **Soil Saturation (Proxy)**: Notice the high saturation index ($>0.85$) generated by the 15-day antecedent monsoon accumulation.
3. **Inspect Explainable Point Factors:**
   - Click anywhere on the red zone near Mundakkai ($11.50^\circ\text{N}, 76.15^\circ\text{E}$).
   - In the right-hand panel, inspect the **Explainable Factor Breakdown**:
     - Rainfall Trigger: $35\%$ contribution (Score: $0.92$)
     - Terrain Slope: $30\%$ contribution (Score: $0.70$)
     - Soil Saturation Proxy: $20\%$ contribution (Score: $0.88$)
     - NASA GLC History: $15\%$ contribution (Score: $0.72$)
     - Note the plain-language explanation: *"Evaluated as VERY_HIGH risk (0.81). The primary hazard driver is Heavy Rainfall..."*
4. **Inspect the Lead-Time Panel:**
   - Note the status: **THRESHOLD BREACHED**. Current intensity exceeds the Caine (1980) critical threshold.
   - Review the scientific limitation disclaimer.
5. **Review Settlement Alert Roster:**
   - In the bottom table, click **Mundakkai** or **Meppadi**.
   - Note the alert state (`CRITICAL`) and simulated evacuation protocol: *"[SIMULATED EVACUATION DISPATCH] CRITICAL: Imminent slope failure risk. Order immediate evacuation of all households in downslope runout zones..."*

## Scenario 2: Chamoli District, Uttarakhand (Himalayan High Relief & Gorges)

### Context
In February 2021, a massive rock/ice collapse and flash-flood debris torrent swept through the Rishiganga and Dhauliganga valleys near Raini and Tapovan.

### Walkthrough Steps:
1. **Switch AOI:** In the top header, select **Chamoli District, Uttarakhand**.
2. **Execution:** The pipeline processes the Himalayan terrain, recalibrating the analysis grid to UTM Zone 44N (`EPSG:32644`) with elevations from $1000\text{m}$ to $4500\text{m}$.
3. **Observe Flash-Flood Dominance:**
   - In the steep Himalayan gorges, note that settlements along the riverbed (Tapovan, Raini) exhibit elevated flash-flood hazard due to upstream drainage convergence.

## Scenario 3: Historical Back-Testing & Model Benchmark

### Objective:
Verify the mandatory comparison: **Rain-Only Baseline vs Full Weighted Risk Index**.

### Walkthrough Steps:
1. In the top navigation bar, click **Back-Testing (GLC)**.
2. Review the comparative metrics table:
   - **ROC-AUC:** Full Weighted Index ($0.972$) outperforms Rain-Only Baseline ($0.583$).
   - **False Alarm Rate (FAR):** Full Model reduces false alarms significantly.
   - **Why?** Rain-only triggers false alarms across flat valley bottoms where rain is high but slope is $0^\circ$. Data fusion with 30m DEM slope and soil-moisture proxy accurately isolates true hazard zones.
3. Review the confusion matrices for hits, false alarms, misses, and correct rejections.
